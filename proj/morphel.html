Why this matters

Tokenizers are often treated as fixed preprocessing. MorpheL makes tokenizer adaptation morphology-aware, helping multilingual models reuse meaningful pieces across inflected and derived word forms when the model cannot fully fine-tune.

The contribution

  • Morpheme-aware adaptation without annotations: MorpheL uses language-specific candidate positions and corpus-level Mutual Information, without gold morpheme boundaries or an external morphological analyzer.
  • Controlled adaptation: The study isolates tokenizer quality across classifier-only, embeddings-plus-classifier, and full fine-tuning regimes using the same XLM-R backbone and 32K vocabulary budget.
  • Beyond classification: An English–Turkish OPUS-100 experiment tests whether the tokenizer transfers to neural machine translation.
  • Reproducible research: The implementation, configurations, and reproducibility artifacts are publicly available.
